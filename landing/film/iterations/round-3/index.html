Round 3: the launch cut

The same real session as One take (select the headline, give it a new color, leave a note, send it, see the diff), cut for a launch instead of a walkthrough. Kinetic type carries the story, and Design Layer's own tools become the motion graphics. The app itself is still never faked: every frame of it is a real capture. The cuts are silent; music comes later.

Option G

Designed live

The type gets edited with the product's own tools. A selection box grabs “it.” and drags it out into the app window. Then each word happens to the real app as you read it: “Select” is selected as the headline is, “Change” turns indigo as the hex is typed, the color floods the frame, and the note pops in as a pin. The edit and the note jump out of the app into the agent's window, the diff plays as giant type, and the finale edits four words.

Option H

Color

The app floats in the dark, in grey. “It works. It looks… fine. It just needs a design layer,” and those two words are the first color in the film. When the new value is entered, the color washes out from the headline across the whole editor and an indigo light comes up behind it. Then the window turns to face the agent's brief, and the change lands in code.

What I took from the references

  • Figma's Config 2024 identity took basic shapes from Figma's toolbar and expanded them into the whole system. Design Layer has the same kind of vocabulary (the selection, its handles and size label, the pin), so G and I use it as the graphics. (It's Nice That, on the Figma Config 2024 identity)
  • Show the product doing one real job with one clear outcome. All three still follow a single edit from start to finish. (Moonb, “13 Software Demo Videos That Sell the Product”)
  • Arrive fast, settle long, and let motion confirm rather than perform. The bigger moves here run on the same expo-out and in-out curves as the explainer. (DesignMD, “Linear vs Vercel: two approaches to minimal motion”)

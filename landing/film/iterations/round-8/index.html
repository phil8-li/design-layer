What I took from Hollywood trailers

  • Trailers are cut in acts: a sparse open, a build, a stop-down, the drop, then a button on the title. Cinematic follows that shape on bars that line up with the cut: 2 bars of dark, 9 bars from the landing to the hover, 3 bars of lift, then the name.
  • The silence before the drop is what makes the drop land. Here the music drops out for a beat as the editor steps aside, then rises quietly into the hover.
  • Trailer music is hybrid: an orchestra plus sound design (Trailer music). Here the strings and horns are composed into the music, and the hits, the boom and the reversed swell are sound design, placed on their frames.
  • Rising tones build tension into a cut. Nolan's films use the Shepard tone, a pitch that seems to rise forever, including in Dunkirk (Shepard tone). One second before the hover is too short for that, so a reversed swell does the job.
  • Commit when the brief says cinematic. I expected the full trailer kit, braams and taiko, to read as an action film; you picked it over the restrained Cinematic.

Notes

  • The music was composed whole from a timed brief: six takes from the same brief. Take 6 was chosen because its sections fall on clean bar lines (8 bars of groove, a 4-bar breakdown, 8 bars of lift), and it rated best in the listening pass.
  • The take was cut to picture on its bar lines, with two seams. One is inside the breakdown, the other between two bars of the lift, each crossfaded over the 30 ms before a beat. It plays 0.4% slower than composed. Its final chord stopped within a beat, so a hall reverb lets it ring.
  • In the listening pass, a multimodal model heard all three mixes five times, in five different orders. Every time, it picked Cinematic as the best answer to your note. Whoosh distraction: 2/10 for Cinematic, 9–10 for round 7. Hollywood feel: 8 against 2–3. Groove: 5–9 against 9 (7 and 8 in the two runs on the final mix). Cheesiness: 1–2. It rated Trailer 10/10 for bombast. Its timestamps were unreliable, so all timing was checked by measurement.
  • Mastered to −16 LUFS, with true peaks under −2.5 dB, as one fixed gain. Round 7's masters had fallen back to a mode that rides the gain and flattens the sections. Round 8 limits first, so the gain stays fixed. The film's audio matches the mix to the sample.

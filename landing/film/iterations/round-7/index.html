Round 7: One move, with sound

The same picture, option T, with three sound designs. Each sits in one of the tempo bands from the Apple breakdown: 74 BPM, regal and cinematic; 100 BPM, smooth and effortless; 120 BPM, elite and kinetic. In all three the sound follows the story and the cuts. The music opens with the landing, holds its breath before the agent's hover and comes back on it, then resolves on the name. Every effect sits on its moment in the picture.

Listen with headphones or good speakers. I can't hear, so everything here was checked by measurement.

1 · Keynote 2 · Effortless 3 · Kinetic
On the beat where it mattersEach tempo was chosen so the editor's landing (4.6 s) and the agent's hover (25.65 s) are a whole number of beats apart. Each track was then nudged under half a beat. A beat now falls on both, within 4 ms.
Effects on their framesClicks fall on the pointer's clicks, air on the camera's moves (loudest where each move is fastest), a settle where a layer lands back, a glass tink where the token lands. A white shimmer pans with the light as it runs around the button. Measured, the effects start on their cues: under a millisecond off, on the median.
The music follows the storyDark and filtered while the line lights, opening as the camera flies through it, steady through the editing, a breakdown as the editor steps aside, the peak on the hover, wide for every page, then resolving. Loudness is measured section by section in each mix.
Restraint, as Apple does itEach effect's level was measured against the music where it plays. Anything masked was brought up, and anything that stood out was taken down. Keynote voices only the turning points; Effortless voices every interaction; Kinetic hits the drops.

Option 1 · 74 BPM · D major / B minor

Keynote

Regal and cinematic: felt piano, warm pads and soft strings, the music leading and the effects few and soft. A low drone sits in the dark under the line, and a swell and a rush of air come as the camera flies through it. The filter opens on a downbeat with a deep impact as the editor lands. The layers breathe as they lift and settle. Air follows each move of the camera, and a glass tink marks the token landing. The note goes out with a two-note chime. Then the music holds its breath and returns on the hover, with a glassy sheen and a white shimmer. A soft D-major bloom arrives with the name.

Option 2 · 100 BPM · F major / D minor

Effortless

Smooth and cool: a relaxed deep-house groove with Rhodes chords and round bass. Every interaction has its own small, dry sound: the clicks, the double-click, the keys as the copy is typed, the detents as the gap opens, the note's pop. The groove starts on the beat as the editor lands. It drops to a breakdown while the editor steps aside and the agent comes in, and comes back on the hover with the sheen and the shimmer. An F-major bloom, a sweep across the name and a click on the button close it.

Option 3 · 120 BPM · C major / A minor

Kinetic

Elite and kinetic: a pulsing arpeggio, a tight kick and claps, glossy stabs, sophisticated rather than hype. A long riser builds through the fly-in and the beat drops with a hit as the editor lands. Typing falls on the 16th notes and clicks are crisp. The token lands with a tink and a double tick. A breakdown and a long riser lead to the second drop, on the hover, as the sheen crosses the button and the light runs around it. A hit opens the board, and the cut ends on a bloom and a sweep across the name.

What I took from Apple's videos, for sound

  • The soundtrack sets the energy, and the tempo picks the feeling: 60–80 BPM regal and cinematic, 90–110 smooth and effortless, 115–123 elite and kinetic. Above that it turns to hype. (leomeethewoo, “Make your product videos look expensive”)
  • Intention: each option has one small palette of sounds and one key, chosen on purpose. No stock “tech” effects and no rap.
  • Sound design is the cherry on top. Avoid effects that don't matter, and remove any that feels too loud or out of place, or that doesn't help people follow the product. Here that was done by measuring every effect against the music.

Notes

  • The music is generated by a real-time music model steered section by section against the film's cues (prompt, density, brightness, drums).
  • Two takes left a narrow noise band at 12–16 kHz above the music, and one also a 14 kHz tone. Both are filtered out, to 90 dB below the music. The stream dropped out for a few tens of milliseconds twice in Effortless and once in Kinetic. Each gap is patched with the same stretch one bar earlier.
  • Mastered to −16 LUFS with peaks under −2.5 dB, AAC at 256 kbps. The film's audio matches the mix to the sample.
